\section{Substrate}
\label{sec:substrate}

Both agents are growing neural cellular automata in the sense of
\citet{mordvintsev2020growing}. A state is a $C \times 32 \times 32$ tensor. Every
cell runs the same update rule on its own $3\times3$ neighbourhood; there is no
central module, no global pooling inside the dynamics, and no cell has a
privileged position. That property is not incidental --- it is what makes the
question of this paper well-posed, because it means damage removes computation
rather than removing a component.

\paragraph{Channels.} We use $C=28$, allocated by role rather than by position:
4 RGBA, 3 sensor, 8 vote, and 13 hidden for the sender. The receiver keeps the
same width with a different split (8 sensor channels for the one-hot symbol, 8
vote channels for its answer, 8 hidden). Channel~3 is alpha and defines
occupancy.

The budget is larger than the 16 channels of \citet{mordvintsev2020growing}, and
deliberately so. Reserving 8 channels for votes out of 16 would leave 4 for
morphogenesis, against the 12 that the original recipe needed for morphology
alone. Capacity starvation of that kind fails silently: the organism trains, the
body looks acceptable, and the protocol never emerges, with nothing in the loss
curve to indicate which of the two is starving.

\paragraph{Perception.} Each channel is convolved with three fixed, non-learned
$3\times3$ kernels --- identity, Sobel-$x$, and Sobel-$y$ (the Sobel kernels
scaled by $\nicefrac{1}{8}$) --- giving an $84$-dimensional perception vector per
cell. The kernels are a registered buffer and never receive gradient.

\paragraph{Update rule.} The perception vector passes through two $1\times1$
convolutions, $84 \to 128 \to 28$, with a ReLU between them. The output is a
\emph{residual} update, gated by a per-cell stochastic fire mask at rate $0.5$,
after which the alive mask is reapplied:
%
\begin{equation}
  x \leftarrow \big(x + f_{\theta}(\mathrm{perceive}(x)) \odot \mathrm{fire}\big)
  \odot \big(\alive_{\mathrm{pre}} \odot \alive_{\mathrm{post}}\big).
\end{equation}
%
A cell is alive if any neighbour in its $3\times3$ window carries
$\alpha > 0.1$, computed as a max-pool; the dilation is what permits outward
growth. Requiring a cell to be alive both before and after the update is what
prevents cells reanimating in isolation.

The second convolution is bias-free and \emph{zero-initialised}, so an untrained
organism is exactly a do-nothing update rule and returns its seed unchanged.
This matters for the death guard described below.

\paragraph{Size.} The whole organism is \textbf{14{,}464 trainable parameters}
(10{,}752 + 128 in the first convolution, 3{,}584 in the second), plus a fixed
756-element perception buffer. Everything reported in this paper --- growing a
body, holding it against perturbation, regenerating from tissue loss, and
carrying a symbol across the body --- is done by that one shared local rule.

\paragraph{Target morphology.} The target is a filled antialiased disk of flat
colour. A disk is used rather than the emoji of
\citet{mordvintsev2020growing} because geodesic distance from the centre is then
analytic and damage location parameterises cleanly as (radius, angle), both of
which the measurements in Section~\ref{sec:protocol} depend on.

Critically, \textbf{the target body is identical for every referent}. The
referent enters only through the sensor channels. If the target varied with the
referent, morphological and semantic recovery would be the same variable and the
hypotheses of this paper would be vacuous rather than merely false.

\paragraph{Training.} Adam at $2\times10^{-3}$, dropping to $2\times10^{-4}$ at
step 2000, with per-parameter-tensor $L_2$ gradient normalisation --- each tensor
normalised by its own norm, which the original work reports as necessary to
prevent late-training loss spikes. Rollout length is drawn per step from
$U[64,96]$. Gradient checkpointing every 8 steps is mandatory rather than an
optimisation: activation memory falls from 3300\,MB to 1110\,MB at $T=64$ on an
8\,GB machine, and is then nearly independent of rollout length (1119\,MB at
$T=128$), which is what makes the long rollouts of Section~\ref{sec:task}
affordable.

Persistence and regeneration are trained with a sample pool of 1024 states: each
step samples 32, ranks them by loss, reseeds the worst 4, and damages the best
12. Damaging the best rather than a random subset is deliberate --- damaging a
pattern that is already failing does not test regeneration.

\paragraph{The death guard.} If every cell dies, the state is identically zero
and the alive mask is a hard threshold with no gradient, so the gradient into the
weights is exactly zero; with per-tensor normalisation, $0/(0+\epsilon)$ stays
zero while Adam's moments decay. The model is then frozen permanently. This is a
trap with a measure-zero escape rather than a local minimum that annealing
escapes, so it is detected explicitly (max alpha below threshold for 3
consecutive steps) and the run is rebuilt from a fresh weight seed with a fresh
optimiser --- stale Adam moments would immediately re-kill it. The guard cannot
fire spuriously because the zero-initialised second convolution returns the seed
exactly. No run reported in this paper triggered it.
