\section{Task and readout}
\label{sec:task}

\subsection{The referential game}

Two organisms play a Lewis signalling game \citep{lewis1969convention}. A sender
observes a referent $r$ drawn uniformly from $\mathcal{R} = \{0,1\}^3$ ($N=8$)
and emits one symbol $m$ from a vocabulary of size $V=8$. A receiver observes the
symbol and nothing else, and must identify $r$.

Communication is necessary \emph{by construction}: the receiver has no other
input path. This is not an assumption to be argued but a property to be verified,
and Section~\ref{sec:protocol} treats it as such --- if removing the message
leaves accuracy above chance, there is a leak and every discrete measure in the
paper is invalid.

Referents are factored into three bits so that attribute-wise mutual information
is well defined. The capacity is matched exactly: $N=8$ needs 3 bits and $V=8$
supplies 3. We never run $N > V^L$; a system reporting 50\% accuracy because 16
referents were forced through an 8-symbol channel has a self-inflicted ceiling
rather than a finding.

\subsection{Injection, and why it stops}

The referent is written into the sender's 3 sensor channels over a central
$3\times3$ patch for the first $T_{\mathrm{inj}} = 16$ steps, and then writing
\emph{stops}. Writing stops rather than zeroing, because zeroing would itself be
an event the organism could detect and time against; stopping is simply removal.

This forces the sender to hold the referent in its own cell state for the rest of
the rollout. The consequence is what makes the damage experiment meaningful: with
continuous injection, a damaged sender simply re-reads the referent, and damage
interrupts a wire. With a closed window, damage can destroy a memory. We report
the continuous-injection variant as a control, because the two experiments are
different and a reader is entitled to know which one was run.

Referent codes are bipolar, in $\{-1,+1\}^3$ rather than $\{0,1\}^3$. Under
$\{0,1\}$ the all-zero code writes nothing and is indistinguishable from no
injection at all.

\subsection{A parameter-free consensus readout}
\label{sec:readout}

The sender's symbol is read out of its 8 vote channels. The readout has
\textbf{zero learned parameters}, and the reason is structural: any learned
readout with a global receptive field could itself solve the task, and the claim
that computation is distributed across the body would become unfalsifiable.

But parameter-free is not the same as decentralised, and this is the sharpest
design problem in the paper. A plain mean over cells has a global receptive field
and instantaneous propagation. Worse, gradient descent actively prefers the
centralised solution: getting a referent to distant cells costs on the order of
64 steps of attenuating backpropagation, while having the 9 sensor cells emit an
enormous vote costs two. \textbf{The shortest gradient path is a megaphone.}

Three mechanisms push back, in increasing order of force.

\paragraph{1. Per-cell softmax before pooling.} Each alive cell emits a point on
the probability simplex, and the pooled result is the uniform mean of those
distributions over the alive set $\alive_t$:
%
\begin{equation}
  \pooled_v \;=\; \frac{1}{|\alive_t|} \sum_{x \in \alive_t}
      \mathrm{softmax}\big(\vote(x)\big)_v ,
  \qquad
  \alive_t(x) = \indicator\!\left[\max_{3\times3} \alpha_t(x) > 0.1\right].
  \label{eq:pool}
\end{equation}
%
Because each cell's contribution is a probability vector, no cell can move the
pooled result by more than $1/|\alive_t|$. The megaphone is excluded
\emph{mechanically}, not detected after the fact by a diagnostic.

We verify this rather than assert it. Driving a single cell's logit to $10^6$ in
favour of one class, against 64 cells mildly preferring another, leaves the
pooled decision unchanged, and silencing that cell moves the pooled distribution
by at most $1/64$. The same input against raw-logit pooling flips the decision to
the screaming cell's class --- a matched negative control confirming that the
bound comes from the per-cell softmax and not from the averaging.

Pooling is a hard-masked uniform mean, never $\alpha$-weighted. Alpha weighting
would make morphological state a multiplicative modulator of the message, so the
message would change during regeneration even if every surviving cell computed
exactly what it did before. That single implementation detail would confound the
entire paper.

\paragraph{2. Per-cell supervision.} Every alive cell is supervised toward the
one-hot target, not merely the pooled result:
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vote}} = \frac{1}{|\alive_t|} \sum_{x \in \alive_t}
    \big\| \mathrm{softmax}(\vote(x)) - \mathbf{e}_{r} \big\|_2^2 .
\end{equation}
%
Consensus then becomes an emergent, measured property rather than an imposed
aggregation, following \citet{randazzo2020selfclassifying}. It also does
something their setting did not need: a distant cell can only vote correctly if
the referent reached it, so supervising the whole body is a direct demand for
propagation. Section~\ref{sec:results-probe} shows why that demand is necessary
here.

The loss is $L_2$ against a one-hot target and never cross-entropy.
\citet{randazzo2020selfclassifying} found that cross-entropy on per-cell logit
channels drives logits upward without bound, so the residual update never decays
and the automaton never settles into a quiescent state. Their reported figures
are 88.1\% of samples reaching full per-cell agreement under $L_2$ against 66.2\%
under cross-entropy at the same step.

\paragraph{3. Readout cell-dropout.} Pooling over a random subset of alive cells
during training is the only mechanism supplying gradient pressure toward
\emph{electorate-invariance} --- the property that the symbol does not depend on
which cells happen to be present. That is precisely what the damage experiment
depends on. \gated{dropout is implemented and unit-tested but is not yet applied
in the training loop; no result in this paper may claim it as an active
mechanism}

\subsection{Deletion-invariance}

The per-cell softmax bounds influence. Consensus does something stronger.

\begin{proposition}[Deletion-invariance under consensus]
\label{prop:deletion}
If $\arg\max_v \vote_v(x) = v^{*}$ for every $x \in \alive$, then for every
nonempty $S \subseteq \alive$,
$\arg\max_v \frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{x \in S} \vote_v(x) = v^{*}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Full consensus means $\vote_{v^{*}}(x) > \vote_u(x)$ for all $x$ and all
$u \neq v^{*}$. Averaging preserves strict inequality termwise.
\end{proof}

The consequence is the backbone of the paper's central confound control: under
full per-cell consensus, \textbf{cell deletion cannot mechanically flip the
symbol}, so any observed symbol change must reflect a genuine change in what the
cells compute. This converts the most dangerous objection --- that the readout is
a mean and deleting 30\% of the cells changes a mean --- from an argument into a
theorem, wherever consensus is high enough to apply.

Consensus is rarely exactly full, so we also report the quantitative version. For
a mean pool, the worst case is dropping the cells that most favour the winner, so
sorting per-cell margins and walking down gives the exact adversarial deletion
tolerance $k^{*}$ in one pass, with no extra forward passes and no search. We
report the quorum fraction $k^{*}/|\alive|$: roughly $0.5$ means genuinely
distributed, roughly $0.02$ means a megaphone.

\subsection{The broadcast precursor}
\label{sec:broadcast}

Between a morphology-only organism and the full game sits a cheaper question that
decides whether the game is viable at all: \emph{can this substrate be taught to
move information across itself?}

The broadcast task isolates it. One organism, no discrete channel, no receiver: a
referent is injected at the centre and every alive cell is supervised to vote for
it. It is the per-cell vote loss of Section~\ref{sec:readout} without the
communication apparatus, and it exists because of the propagation measurement in
Section~\ref{sec:results-probe}. Its results are in
Section~\ref{sec:results-broadcast}.
